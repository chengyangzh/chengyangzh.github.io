I study how intelligent systems **discover, represent, and reuse structure**. I am especially interested in cases where surface similarity is not enough: relational reasoning, analogical transfer, language change, and human–AI differences in perception and judgment.

I am an undergraduate at **UCLA**, studying Cognitive Science with a Computing specialization and Linguistics & Computer Science. My work moves between artificial intelligence, computational linguistics, and cognitive science; I like turning a difficult conceptual question into a representation, an experiment, and an evaluation that can actually fail.

### questions I keep returning to

- Can a model retrieve the *right precedent* because two problems share a relational structure rather than similar words?
- What abstractions make historical language change computationally reconstructible without flattening philological knowledge?
- When humans and models reach similar answers, are they using the same information—or only producing the same output?

### selected work

**[Relational reasoning in vision-language models](/projects/relational-reasoning-vlms/)**  
Tracing relation-specific computation in vision-language models, including chains of spatial and agentic relations and function-vector style interventions.

**[Category-based historical phonology](/projects/historical-phonology/)**  
A philologist-inspired optimization framework for reconstructing sound systems across historical time planes using categories, distinctive features, reflexes, and structured constraints.

**[Echo: structure-first analogy for agents](/projects/echo/)**  
An agent prototype that represents problems through goals, constraints, relations, and causal structure before searching for useful analogies.

**[Perception and human–AI comparison](/projects/stereokinetic-depth/)**  
VR psychophysics and recognition experiments that operationalize noisy human behavior into measurable computational tests.
