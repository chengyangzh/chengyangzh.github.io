My research is organized less by application area than by a recurring problem: **how does an intelligent system recover the hidden structure of a task?** I approach that question from several directions.

## Relational and analogical reasoning

I am interested in representations that preserve *relations* rather than only semantic similarity. Current work includes analogical and relational reasoning in vision-language models, relation chains across multiple entities, and interventions that probe which attention heads carry relation-specific information. In parallel, I built **Echo**, an agent prototype that retrieves precedents through goals, constraints, causal roles, and relational structure.

## Computational language and language change

Historical phonology is a useful stress test for structured inference: the evidence is sparse, heterogeneous, and filtered through expert categories. With Prof. Weiwei Sun, I am developing category-based reconstruction methods that combine distinctive features, fanqie and rhyme-table structure, modern dialect reflexes, Sino-Xenic evidence, and cross-period constraints. I also work on multilingual language-model behavior, surprisal, animacy, and syntax–semantics interactions.

## Human–AI comparison

A model can match human accuracy while relying on very different evidence. I use behavioral experiments and model evaluation to make that mismatch visible. My work has included face-privacy distortions evaluated across human participants and multiple recognition models, as well as VR experiments on stereokinetic depth perception.

## Methodological taste

I am drawn to projects where the key contribution is not just a larger model or dataset, but a sharper *operationalization*: choosing the right unit of representation, building a test that isolates the mechanism, and designing an evaluation that distinguishes plausible explanations.
